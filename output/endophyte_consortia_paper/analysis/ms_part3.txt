# 4. Discussion

## 4.1 What salinity did to IR-64 seedlings

The uninoculated plants behaved much as the literature predicts for a salt-susceptible *indica* cultivar. Growth slowed first and most visibly in the roots. At 150 mM, root length fell by about half and SVI by 57.7%, which fits the osmotic phase described by Munns and Tester (2008): water uptake becomes harder, cell expansion slows, and growth drops long before ions reach toxic levels in the leaves. The pigment data point to damage beyond osmotic slowdown. Chlorophyll *a* lost more than two-thirds of its value at only 50 mM, whereas germination at that level barely changed (80.0% vs. 83.3%). Pigment loss that steep and that early usually signals oxidative injury in the chloroplast (Gill & Tuteja, 2010). The simultaneous fall in carotenoids, which normally quench excess excitation energy, would only have made the chloroplast more vulnerable.

The metabolite results are a little less conventional. Salt-tolerant plants typically accumulate soluble sugars as osmolytes under stress (Parida & Das, 2005; Mahajan & Tuteja, 2005). In IR-64, soluble sugars moved the other way, falling by 67.5% at 150 mM, and protein and phenolics fell with them. We read this as a sign that the susceptible cultivar could not keep carbon assimilation going well enough to fund an osmotic response. With chlorophyll *a* down by 83%, less assimilate was available for sugars, amino acids and phenylpropanoid products. This interpretation is consistent with reviews that place photosynthetic capacity upstream of most other salt-tolerance traits in rice (Saleem et al., 2025; Sackey et al., 2025), but our data cannot prove the causal order. Leaf Na^{+} and K^{+}, gas exchange and proline were not measured, and each would help separate these explanations.

## 4.2 Germination was not where inoculation made its difference

One of the clearest outcomes of this study is a negative one. Across 25 seeds on filter paper and 30 seeds per pot, no inoculant changed germination by a statistically detectable amount. Many seed bacterization studies report higher germination after inoculation (Sagar et al., 2020; Ali et al., 2022), and our direction of effect agrees with them. IS-06 and C4-5 germinated 10 to 17 percentage points above the control at 150 mM. But with 30 seeds per treatment, Fisher's exact test needs a difference of about 27 percentage points (for example, 60% against 87%) to reach significance, so our design was never going to confirm effects of the size we saw. We'd rather say that plainly than lean on percentages that could easily shrink with more seeds.

The distinction matters for how bacterization is sold to farmers. Salt at 150 mM cut germination by 28%, root length by 52% and chlorophyll *a* by 83%. Germination was therefore one of the least salt-sensitive traits we measured, and the stand losses that matter in saline paddies are more likely to come from weak seedlings than from seeds that never sprout. That is where the inoculants worked. Singh et al. (2021) point out that rice is most vulnerable during seedling establishment, not at imbibition, and our data fit that pattern. There is also a caveat on timing. Salt was applied from the two- to three-leaf stage onwards, so salinity effects on emergence should have been small; the decline we recorded suggests that early saline irrigation reached the seed zone sooner than planned, or that the counts partly reflect seedling survival. Future trials should record emergence and survival separately.

## 4.3 IS-06: a strong single strain for pigment and osmolyte protection

IS-06 was the most consistent performer. Under salt, it ranked first among the isolates for chlorophyll *a*, soluble protein, soluble sugars and DPPH activity at every NaCl level, first or joint first for germination, and it topped the CMI at all four salinity levels. The size of its effect on chlorophyll *a*, a 240% gain over the control at 150 mM, is on the high side of what has been reported for rice. Halotolerant *Bacillus* strains, for instance, restored chlorophyll and photosynthetic parameters of salt-stressed rice alongside better ion homeostasis and antioxidant defence (Siddika et al., 2024). Halotolerant endophytes have done the same while adjusting hormone levels and the expression of ion transporter genes (Asif et al., 2023).

The mechanism behind IS-06 is still unknown, because the isolates were not characterized for growth-promoting traits here. Two routes seem most likely. The first is ACC deaminase. By lowering stress ethylene, it would protect root elongation and delay ethylene-driven leaf senescence and chlorophyll breakdown (Glick, 2014); rice seedlings inoculated with ACC deaminase-positive *Enterobacter* strains showed exactly this kind of growth protection (Sarkar et al., 2018; Sagar et al., 2020). The second route is better ionic control. If IS-06 reduced sodium entry into the shoot, as reported for other rice endophytes (M. A. Khan et al., 2021; Asif et al., 2023), the chloroplast would face less ionic stress and pigment synthesis could continue. The two routes aren't mutually exclusive, and tests for ACC deaminase activity, indole-3-acetic acid production, exopolysaccharide synthesis and shoot Na^{+}/K^{+} ratios would tell them apart.

The soluble sugar data add something. IS-06 plants kept 89% of their unstressed sugar level at 150 mM, whereas uninoculated plants kept 33%. Soluble sugars are both products of photosynthesis and osmolytes in their own right, so this result probably reflects the preserved chlorophyll feeding a steady carbon supply. Inoculation-driven accumulation of compatible solutes has been shown directly in rice (Arora et al., 2020; Ramasamy & Mahawar, 2023). Our data fit that picture, but they can't show whether the extra sugar was used for osmotic adjustment or simply reflects higher assimilation.

## 4.4 Consortia: complementarity is real but depends on the pair

The meta-analytic expectation is that consortia outperform single strains (Liu et al., 2023). Our results support a more guarded version of that claim. On the CMI averaged across salinity levels, only one consortium, C4-5, beat both of its parents. C2-5 sat between its parents, and C1-3, C1-5 and C2-3 ranked below both of theirs. Two consortia even fell below the uninoculated control on the average CMI. Mixing strains, then, isn't automatically an improvement.

C4-5 deserves a closer look, because it combined IS-04 and IS-05, the third- and second-ranked single isolates. At 150 mM, C4-5 exceeded both parents for soluble protein (181.51 vs. 133.45 and 138.26 µg mL^{−1}), chlorophyll *a* (15.6 vs. 12.5 and 11.6 mg g^{−1} FW) and SVI (2246 vs. 1766 and 2010). It fell below both for soluble sugars (70.08 vs. 78.77 and 82.25 µg mL^{−1}). A consortium that improves some traits beyond either parent, but not all, is what complementarity looks like in practice. Each partner probably contributes a different mechanism, and the combination covers more of the stress response than either strain alone. Consortium inoculation has likewise improved growth and yield of wheat under saline conditions (M. Y. Khan et al., 2022). Liu et al. (2023) linked the advantage of consortia partly to functional diversity among the members.

Why did the other pairs fail? The in vitro compatibility test was passed by all five, so the answer isn't simple antagonism on agar. Two explanations seem plausible. Strains that share a niche inside the root may compete for the same colonization sites, and the partner with weaker plant benefits may win (Compant et al., 2019; Kandasamy & Kathirvel, 2023). Alternatively, the same trait may be duplicated in both partners, adding cost to the plant without adding function. The pigment data offer a hint. Each of the three weak consortia contains at least one of the three isolates that protected chlorophyll least (IS-01, IS-02 and IS-03), two of them pair two such isolates, and all three lowered chlorophyll *a* below the uninoculated control. The pattern isn't absolute: C2-5 pairs IS-02 with IS-05 and still protected chlorophyll *a*. Even so, if a poor partner can dilute a good one, mixing adds risk as well as potential.

We should add a qualification. The consortium and isolate experiments were run as separate batches with a shared uninoculated control, and the chlorophyll *b* and DPPH data show that batch effects existed. Comparisons between consortia and their parent isolates are therefore indirect. A single experiment testing each consortium next to its own two parents would give a cleaner test of synergy, and we see that as the logical next step.

## 4.5 Protein, phenolics and antioxidant activity

Protein retention was the one trait where consortia clearly outperformed single strains. C4-5 and C2-5 retained 81% and 75% of their unstressed protein at 150 mM, against 70% for IS-06 and 40% for uninoculated plants. Soluble protein reflects both synthesis and breakdown. Under salinity, poorer nitrogen assimilation and faster proteolysis both push it down (Parida & Das, 2005). A consortium with two partners could plausibly ease both processes, for example if one partner improves nitrogen supply while the other limits oxidative damage to proteins. Our data don't settle which process dominated. The strong protein–sugar correlation (r = 0.89) suggests that the two traits share a common driver, most likely the overall carbon and nitrogen status of the leaf.

Phenolics behaved like a stress-protective trait that the susceptible cultivar could not sustain on its own. Uninoculated leaves lost 70% of their phenolic content at 150 mM, while C4-5 kept its phenolic level near the unstressed control at 50 mM, and IS-06 did the same up to 100 mM. Phenolic compounds scavenge radicals and stabilize membranes, and bacterial inoculation is known to raise antioxidant capacity in salt-stressed plants (Neshat et al., 2022). The sharp drop under IS-01 at 150 mM (2.80 µg mL^{−1}) is an unexpected outlier. It suggests that some endophytes can suppress phenolic accumulation, and that such strains shouldn't be carried forward into formulations.

DPPH activity was the least informative trait. The unstressed control differed between batches (90% vs. 70.6%), activity in uninoculated plants was not monotonic with salt, and the trait correlated only weakly with the other markers. DPPH measures the combined radical-quenching capacity of an extract, not the activity of any one enzyme (Gülçin, 2020). It can therefore move for reasons unrelated to stress protection. Phenolic constituents and antioxidant activity of rice also respond to other abiotic stresses (Bhadwal et al., 2024), which makes the assay a broad rather than a specific marker. The one pattern that did hold was that IS-06 and C4-5 gave the highest scavenging at 150 mM. We would treat DPPH as supporting evidence here and not as a primary endpoint. Measuring superoxide dismutase, catalase and ascorbate peroxidase activities directly would give a sharper picture of the antioxidant response.

## 4.6 What the indices and PCA add

Ranking inoculants trait by trait gives different winners depending on which trait is chosen: IS-06 for sugars, C4-5 for protein, IS-05 for root length. The CMI and the PCA resolve this by asking which treatments stay near the top across the whole profile. Both methods agreed on the first two places, IS-06 and C4-5, and on the bottom of the ranking. That agreement matters because the two methods weight traits differently. The CMI weights every trait equally, whereas PCA weights them by how much they vary together. Negrão et al. (2017) argue that composite, relative measures are the most defensible way to compare salt responses when baselines differ, and our experience supports that view.

PC1 captured half the variance and loaded positively on every trait. In other words, most of what distinguished good and poor treatments was a single, general axis of seedling health, rather than trade-offs between traits. PC2 then separated osmolyte-type traits (protein and sugars) from root and shoot length and DPPH activity. On this axis, the consortia and the best single isolates took different routes to the same outcome. The consortia leaned towards protein retention, while IS-06 leaned towards growth and pigment protection. Jolliffe and Cadima (2016) note that components with modest variance can still carry biologically meaningful structure, and PC2 seems to do so here.

## 4.7 Limitations and next steps

Several limitations need to be stated. First, the isolates were not identified to species, and their growth-promoting traits were not screened. Without 16S rRNA gene sequencing, the strains can't be compared with published inoculants or registered as bioinoculants. That work is essential before any field release (Afzal et al., 2019; Ganie et al., 2021). Second, biochemical values are expressed per millilitre of extract. The fixed tissue-to-solvent ratio makes treatments comparable, but it prevents direct comparison with studies that report per gram fresh weight. The absolute chlorophyll values should also be checked against a per-gram calculation before they are compared with other studies. Third, several assays recorded identical replicate SDs across all treatments (±0.15). This probably reflects analytical rather than biological replication, which would inflate the F values in Table 3; the effect sizes and ranking are more reliable than the exact *p*-values. Fourth, colonization was not verified. Re-isolating the inoculated strains from surface-sterilized roots and leaves, or tracking them with marker genes, would confirm that the effects came from endophytic populations (Walitang et al., 2017; Gupta et al., 2023). Finally, the trial ended at 45 DAS in pots. Salt tolerance at the seedling stage correlates only weakly with tolerance at flowering in rice (Singh et al., 2021), so yield-stage field trials remain the real test.

Despite these limits, the study offers a practical screening sequence: a compatibility check, a laboratory vigour assay, a graded-salinity pot trial and composite ranking. That sequence narrowed eleven candidate inoculants to two. For IS-06 and C4-5, we'd now prioritize strain identification, quantification of growth-promoting traits, a direct consortium-versus-parent comparison in one experiment, and multi-location field testing in salt-affected rice fields. Coupling inoculation with a tolerant cultivar is also worth testing, since host genotype shapes the endophytic community (Walitang et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2025).

# 5. Conclusions

Salinity at 150 mM NaCl reduced seedling vigour of IR-64 rice by 58%, chlorophyll *a* by 83% and soluble sugars by 68%. Bacterization with rice endophytes didn't change germination significantly, but it strongly protected seedling growth, photosynthetic pigments, soluble protein, sugars and phenolics under salt. Isolate IS-06 was the most consistent single strain. At 150 mM it raised chlorophyll *a* by 240%, soluble sugars by 169% and SVI by 142% over uninoculated plants. Consortium C4-5 retained 81% of its unstressed protein and exceeded both of its parent strains for protein, chlorophyll *a* and vigour. Three of the five consortia, however, ranked below both of their parent strains, so the benefit of combining strains depended on which strains were combined. A composite membership index and principal component analysis agreed on IS-06 and C4-5 as the leading candidates. The next steps are to identify these strains, confirm that they colonize the plant, and test them at the yield stage in saline fields.
