# 4. Discussion

## 4.1 Salinity damage in IR-64 seedlings

The uninoculated plants behaved as expected for a salt-susceptible *indica* cultivar. Roots slowed first: at 150 mM, root length fell by about half and SVI by 57.7%, which fits the osmotic phase in which water uptake and cell expansion are restricted before ions reach toxic levels (Munns & Tester, 2008). The pigment data point beyond osmotic slowdown. Chlorophyll *a* lost more than two-thirds of its value at only 50 mM, while germination barely changed, and pigment loss that steep and that early usually signals oxidative injury in the chloroplast (Gill & Tuteja, 2010). The parallel fall in carotenoids, which quench excess excitation energy, would have left the chloroplast more exposed still.

Soluble sugars behaved less conventionally. Tolerant plants usually accumulate sugars as osmolytes (Parida & Das, 2005; Mahajan & Tuteja, 2005), yet in IR-64 sugars fell by 67.5%, together with protein and phenolics. This pattern is interpreted as a susceptible cultivar unable to keep carbon assimilation going well enough to fund an osmotic response, consistent with reviews that place photosynthetic capacity upstream of most salt-tolerance traits in rice (Sackey et al., 2025) and with the broader view of salt signalling as a reprogramming of metabolism (Zhu, 2016). The present data cannot establish the causal order, because gas exchange was not measured.

## 4.2 Germination was not where inoculation made its difference

The clearest negative result is that no inoculant changed germination detectably. Many bacterization studies report higher germination (Sagar et al., 2020; Ali et al., 2022), and the direction of effect for the best inoculants agrees, with IS-06 and C4-5 10–17 percentage points above the control at 150 mM (Fig. 2), although IS-02, IS-03 and C2-3 fell below it. But with 30 seeds per treatment, Fisher's exact test needs a difference of about 27 points to reach significance, so the design could not confirm effects of this size. Germination was also one of the least salt-sensitive traits (SIR 28%, against 52% for root length and 83% for chlorophyll *a*). Stand losses in saline paddies are therefore more likely to come from weak seedlings than from seeds that never sprout, which is where the inoculants worked and where rice is most vulnerable (Singh et al., 2021). Because salt was applied from 15 DAS, its effect on emergence suggests that saline irrigation reached the seed zone early or that the counts partly reflect seedling survival; future trials should record the two separately.

## 4.3 IS-06: a strong single strain for pigment and osmolyte protection

IS-06 was the most consistent performer, ranking first among the isolates for chlorophyll *a*, protein, sugars and DPPH under salt and first on the CMI at all four salinity levels. Its 240% gain in chlorophyll *a* at 150 mM is at the high end of reports for rice, where halotolerant *Bacillus* strains restored photosynthetic parameters alongside ion homeostasis and antioxidant defence (Siddika et al., 2024) and halotolerant endophytes adjusted hormones and ion-transporter expression (Asif et al., 2023).

The mechanism behind IS-06 is still open, and two routes seem most likely. ACC deaminase would lower stress ethylene, protecting root elongation and delaying ethylene-driven chlorophyll breakdown (Glick, 2014); ACC deaminase-positive *Enterobacter* strains protected salt-stressed rice seedlings in exactly this way (Sarkar et al., 2018; Sagar et al., 2020). Alternatively, IS-06 may limit sodium entry into the shoot, as reported for other rice endophytes (M. A. Khan et al., 2021), possibly helped by exopolysaccharides that bind Na^{+} around the roots (Sun et al., 2020). Most growth-promoting strains combine several such traits (Glick, 2012; Etesami & Maheshwari, 2018), and direct assays for ACC deaminase, indole-3-acetic acid, exopolysaccharide and shoot Na^{+}/K^{+} would separate them. The sugar data add one point: IS-06 kept 89% of its unstressed sugar against 33% in the control, which probably reflects preserved chlorophyll feeding a steady carbon supply. Inoculation-driven accumulation of compatible solutes is well documented (Arora et al., 2020; Ramasamy & Mahawar, 2023), but the present data cannot show whether the extra sugar served osmotic adjustment or simply reflects higher assimilation.

## 4.4 Consortia: complementarity is real but depends on the pair

Meta-analysis predicts that consortia outperform single strains (Liu et al., 2023); the present results support a more guarded version. On the average CMI, only C4-5 beat both of its parents, C2-5 sat between its parents, and C1-3, C1-5 and C2-3 ranked below both. C4-5 combined the second- and third-ranked isolates and, at 150 mM, exceeded both parents for protein (181.51 vs. 133.45 and 138.26 µg mL^{−1}), chlorophyll *a* and SVI, though not for sugars. Improving some traits beyond either parent, but not all, is what complementarity looks like in practice, and consortia have likewise improved growth and yield of wheat under salinity (M. Y. Khan et al., 2022).

All five pairs passed the plate test, so the failures are not simple antagonism. Partners sharing a niche inside the root may compete for colonization sites (Compant et al., 2019; Kandasamy & Kathirvel, 2023), or the same trait may be duplicated without adding function. Each weak consortium contains at least one of the three isolates that protected chlorophyll least (IS-01, IS-02, IS-03), although C2-5 shows the pattern is not absolute. Mixing therefore adds risk as well as potential, which is why reviews of halotolerant bacteria stress trait-based partner selection (Numan et al., 2018; Zamanzadeh-Nasrabadi et al., 2023). Because isolates and consortia were run as separate batches with a shared control, consortium–parent comparisons are indirect and should be confirmed in a single experiment.

## 4.5 Protein, phenolics and antioxidant activity

Protein retention was the one trait where consortia clearly outperformed single strains (81% for C4-5 and 75% for C2-5, against 70% for IS-06 and 40% for the control). Salinity lowers soluble protein through poorer nitrogen assimilation and faster proteolysis (Parida & Das, 2005), and two partners could plausibly ease both. Phenolics, which scavenge radicals and stabilize membranes, were largely maintained by C4-5 and IS-06, in line with inoculation-induced antioxidant capacity in salt-stressed plants (Neshat et al., 2022); the collapse under IS-01 at 150 mM suggests that some endophytes suppress phenolic accumulation and should not be carried forward. DPPH was the least informative trait: it differed between batches, was not monotonic with salt and measures an extract's combined radical-quenching capacity rather than any one enzyme (Gülçin, 2020). Phenolic and antioxidant profiles of rice also shift under other abiotic stresses (Bhadwal et al., 2024), so DPPH is supporting evidence here and not a primary endpoint.

## 4.6 What the indices and PCA add

Ranking trait by trait gives different winners: IS-06 for sugars, C4-5 for protein, IS-05 for root length. The CMI and PCA, which weight traits differently, agreed on IS-06 and C4-5 at the top and on C2-3 and C1-3 at the bottom, supporting composite, relative measures for comparing salt responses (Negrão et al., 2017). PC1 showed that most of the difference between good and poor treatments lay on a single axis of seedling health, while PC2 separated the consortia's protein-retention route from IS-06's growth-and-pigment route.

## 4.7 Limitations and next steps

The isolates still need confirmed taxonomic identity and quantified growth-promoting traits before these isolates can be compared with published inoculants or registered (Afzal et al., 2019; Ganie et al., 2021). Biochemical values are expressed per volume of extract; the fixed tissue-to-solvent ratio makes treatments comparable, but not directly with per-gram data from other studies. Several assays recorded identical replicate SDs (±0.15), probably analytical rather than biological replication, which inflates F; effect sizes and rankings are the more reliable guide. Colonization was not verified by re-isolation or marker tracking (Walitang et al., 2017; Gupta et al., 2023), and ion contents, oxidative-damage markers and antioxidant enzymes were outside the scope of this trial. Finally, the trial ended at 45 DAS in pots, and seedling-stage tolerance in rice correlates only weakly with tolerance at flowering (Singh et al., 2021). Salt-tolerant rhizobacteria have improved rice yield in saline soil (Shultana et al., 2020), so yield-stage field testing of IS-06 and C4-5, ideally with a tolerant cultivar since host genotype shapes the endophytic community (Walitang et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2025), is the logical next step.

# 5. Conclusions

Salinity at 150 mM NaCl reduced seedling vigour of IR-64 rice by 58%, chlorophyll *a* by 83% and soluble sugars by 68%. Bacterization with rice endophytes did not change germination significantly, but it strongly protected seedling growth, photosynthetic pigments, soluble protein, sugars and phenolics under salt. IS-06 was the most consistent single strain, raising chlorophyll *a* by 240%, sugars by 169% and SVI by 142% at 150 mM. C4-5 retained 81% of its unstressed protein and exceeded both parent strains for protein, chlorophyll *a* and vigour, whereas three of the five consortia ranked below both parents. A composite index and PCA agreed on IS-06 and C4-5 as the leading candidates for strain identification, colonization tests and yield-stage evaluation in saline fields.
