TITLE: Endophytic Bacterial Consortia from Cereal Crops: Characterization of
Secondary Metabolites and Bioprospecting for Plant Growth Promotion under
Salinity Stress

AFFILIATION: Plant Microbiology and Stress Physiology Laboratory

================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Salinity represents one of the most significant abiotic stressors limiting rice
(Oryza sativa L.) productivity globally, affecting approximately 800 million
hectares of agricultural land. Endophytic bacteria possess multifarious plant
growth-promoting traits that can mitigate salt-induced physiological stress
through complementary mechanisms. This study evaluates the efficacy of six
endophytic bacterial isolates and their consortia in promoting rice germination,
seedling vigor, and biochemical resilience under graded salinity stress
(0–150 mM NaCl). Seed bacterization experiments demonstrated that isolate
IS-06 produced the highest germination percentage (92–93%) across salinity
levels, followed by consortium C4-5 (90%). Under salt stress, IS-06 inoculation
increased chlorophyll content by 289%, soluble protein by 56%, phenolic
compounds by 236%, and DPPH radical scavenging activity by 26%, relative to
uninoculated controls. Multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) revealed
significant treatment effects on the combined physiological endpoints
(F₁₁,₃₂ = 8.47, p < 0.001). Analysis of variance (ANOVA) demonstrated
significant salinity-by-inoculation interactions for chlorophyll (F₃,₃₂ = 14.22,
p < 0.001), protein (F₃,₃₂ = 9.81, p < 0.001), and antioxidant activity
(F₃,₃₂ = 11.56, p < 0.001). The consortium C4-5 exhibited synergistic effects,
maintaining 21% higher protein levels than the best individual isolate under
150 mM NaCl. Coefficient of variation analysis indicated that IS-06 and C4-5
reduced physiological trait variance under stress conditions (CV reduction:
18–34%), suggesting superior stress buffering capacity. These findings
establish endophytic bacteria as viable bioinoculants for salinity-resilient
rice cultivation, with direct applications to sustainable agriculture in
salt-affected regions.

Keywords: endophytic bacteria, plant growth promotion, salinity stress,
secondary metabolites, bioactive compounds, sustainable agriculture

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) constitutes the primary staple carbohydrate source for
over 3.5 billion individuals worldwide, particularly across Asia-Pacific regions
where per capita consumption exceeds 150 kg per annum [1]. Global rice
production demands careful optimization, as the crop must serve as both a
nutritional foundation and an economic driver for rural development in
low-income countries [2]. However, inherent physiological limitations—including
narrow ionic tolerance and shallow root architecture—render rice cultivation
highly vulnerable to environmental perturbations, particularly soil salinity [1,
3].

The global extent of salt-affected soils has reached unprecedented proportions.
Quantitative surveys indicate that 802 million hectares of land experience
salinity-induced productivity loss, with annual expansion rates of 1.0–2.0%,
and approximately 20% of all irrigated agricultural land now exhibits
electrical conductivity exceeding 4 dS/m [2, 4]. In South Asia, particularly
the Indo-Gangetic Plain of India, salinity-attributed losses in rice production
reach 45% relative to non-saline benchmarks, translating into an estimated loss
of 16.8 million tonnes annually and unprecedented food security implications [5,
6].

Salinity stress initiates injury through three complementary mechanisms: osmotic
disruption of water potential gradients reduces cellular hydration and turgor;
ionic toxicity from sodium and chloride accumulation disrupts enzyme function
and metabolic homeostasis; and oxidative damage ensues from reactive oxygen
species (ROS) overproduction in chloroplasts and mitochondria [7, 8]. These
multifactorial stressors coordinately suppress germination percentage, root
elongation, chlorophyll synthesis, nutrient acquisition, and photosynthetic
capacity, ultimately constraining yield formation [3, 9].

Conventional approaches to salinity mitigation—including crop selection, soil
amendment, and irrigation management—offer limited cost-effectiveness in
resource-constrained agricultural systems [2]. Consequently, microbial
biotechnology presents a compelling alternative. Endophytic bacteria, defined as
viable microbial cells residing asymptomatically within plant tissues, represent
an untapped reservoir of functional diversity capable of complementing the host
stress-response apparatus [10, 11]. Rice endosphere communities are taxonomically
diverse, comprising cultivable taxa including Bacillus, Pseudomonas,
Enterobacter, Burkholderia, Pantoea, Serratia, and Microbacterium [12, 13].
These endophytes interact with the host through multiple pathways: phytohormone
production (indole-3-acetic acid, gibberellins), nutrient solubilization,
siderophore synthesis, and crucially, 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylate (ACC)
deaminase activity that attenuates stress-induced ethylene [14, 15].

The literature establishes several empirically validated mechanisms through which
endophytic bacteria mitigate salinity injury. First, ACC deaminase-producing
endophytes catalyze hydrolysis of ACC, the immediate ethylene precursor, thereby
suppressing the deleterious stress-ethylene surge that otherwise inhibits root
elongation [16, 17]. Second, ion homeostasis modulation—including selective
upregulation of genes encoding salt overly sensitive (SOS) family transporters
(OsNHX1, OsSOS1, OsHKT1) and potassium acquisition proteins—restores the Na⁺/K⁺
balance critical for enzyme function [18, 19]. Third, osmoprotectant synthesis
and accumulation of compatible solutes (proline, glycine betaine, trehalose)
sustain cellular turgor under osmotic stress [20, 21]. Fourth, upregulation of
enzymatic antioxidants (superoxide dismutase, catalase, peroxidase, ascorbate
peroxidase, phenylalanine ammonia-lyase) and accumulation of non-enzymatic
scavengers (phenolic compounds, carotenoids) mitigate oxidative damage [22, 23].
Fifth, exopolysaccharide (EPS) secretion by endophytes chelates sodium ions in
the rhizosphere and protects root surfaces [24, 25]. Finally, modulation of
phytohormonal signaling networks—including abscisic acid, salicylic acid, and
jasmonic acid—coordinates systemic stress responses [19, 26].

Critical gaps remain in the literature. While single-isolate inoculation studies
demonstrate efficacy, meta-analytical evidence indicates that microbial consortia
combining compatible strains achieve superior performance (average growth increase
of 48% versus 29% for single-species inoculation) [27, 28]. However, integrative
studies directly comparing individual endophytic isolates with their consortium
across sequential growth stages (germination, seedling establishment, biochemical
endpoints) in a unified experimental framework remain scarce [29]. Moreover,
quantitative assessment of synergistic interactions within consortia, expressed
through variance reduction metrics and multivariate physiological responses, has
not been systematically evaluated in rice [27, 30].

This investigation addresses these gaps by: (i) isolating endophytic bacteria
from rice germplasm adapted to moderately saline environments; (ii) constructing
compatible microbial consortia and evaluating their biochemical compatibility;
(iii) conducting seed bacterization experiments to assess germination promotion
under laboratory conditions; (iv) evaluating individual isolates and consortia
under progressive salinity in controlled greenhouse settings; (v) quantifying
secondary metabolite accumulation (chlorophyll, phenolics, protein, sugars,
antioxidant activity) at multiple salinity thresholds; and (vi) applying
multivariate statistical methods (MANOVA, ANOVA, variance partitioning) to
establish the efficacy hierarchy and identify synergistic interactions. The
overarching objective is to develop evidence-based bioinoculant formulations for
salt-affected rice cultivation.

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS
================================================================================

2.1 Bacterial Consortium Preparation and Compatibility Assessment

Six endophytic bacterial isolates (IS-01 through IS-06) previously isolated from
rice root and leaf tissues were selected based on preliminary ACC deaminase
activity screening. Isolates were cultured individually in nutrient broth (NB,
Merck) under aseptic conditions at 37 ± 1 °C with 120 rpm orbital agitation for
24 hours until attainment of exponential phase (OD₆₀₀ = 0.8–1.0). Five consortia
(C1-3, C1-5, C2-3, C2-5, C4-5) were formulated by combining isolate pairs in
equal volumetric proportions (1:1 v/v). Each consortium was homogenized via
gentle vortexing for 2 minutes to ensure uniform distribution. Consortium purity
and viability were confirmed by quadrant streak plating onto sterile nutrient
agar, followed by 24-hour incubation at 37 ± 1 °C. Colony morphology, pigmentation,
and growth characteristics were recorded to verify absence of antagonistic
interactions.

2.2 Seed Surface Sterilization Protocol

Healthy, uniform seeds of the salt-susceptible rice cultivar IR-64 were obtained
from certified seed sources. Surface sterilization was performed according to
established microbiological protocols with slight modification. Seeds were
immersed in 95% ethanol (v/v) with gentle swirling for 45 seconds, followed by
5% sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) treatment for 8 minutes. Seeds were subsequently
rinsed three times with sterile distilled water to remove residual sterilant and
air-dried on sterile filter paper.

2.3 In Vitro Seed Germination Assays (Petri-plate Method)

Sterile Whatman No. 1 filter paper was moistened with the assigned treatment
solution and placed in sterile Petri dishes (9 cm diameter). Twenty-five
surface-sterilized seeds were arranged uniformly on the filter paper. Treatment
groups included: (i) control (0 mM NaCl); (ii) individual isolates (IS-01 to
IS-06) at ≈10⁸ CFU mL⁻¹; (iii) graded salinity (50, 100, 150 mM NaCl); and
(iv) isolate/consortium + salinity combinations. Petri plates were maintained at
25 ± 2 °C under 12-hour photoperiod (500 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹). Filter paper
moisture was maintained daily through addition of distilled water or treatment
solution. Germination (radicle emergence ≥2 mm) was recorded daily for 7 days.
At experiment termination, root and shoot lengths were measured with calipers
and recorded to the nearest 1 mm. Seedling vigor index (SVI) was calculated as:
SVI = germination percentage × mean seedling length (cm).

2.4 Greenhouse (Pot Culture) Evaluation under Progressive Salinity

The experimental design was a factorial arrangement of treatments in a completely
randomized block design (CRD) with three replicate pots per treatment. The potting
medium consisted of sterilized black soil and coco-peat mixed in 1:2 (w/w) ratio
and sterilized by autoclaving at 121 °C, 15 psi for 45 minutes. Polyethylene
pots (15 cm diameter × 18 cm height) were filled with 2.5 kg of potting medium
and arranged in a greenhouse maintained at 28 ± 2 °C with natural photoperiod
and 60–70% relative humidity.

Bacterized seeds were sown at a density of 5 seeds per pot and thinned to 3
uniform plants at the 2-leaf stage (8 days after sowing). Salt stress was
imposed at the 2–3 leaf stage (15 days after sowing) through alternate-day
irrigation with 500 mL of the designated NaCl solution (0, 50, 100, or 150 mM).
Salt stress was maintained for 30 consecutive days. Plants were irrigated with
deionized water on intermediate days to maintain consistent soil moisture. Plants
were harvested 45 days after sowing (30 days post-stress imposition).

2.6 Biochemical Estimation Protocols

2.6.1 Chlorophyll Quantification

Fresh leaf tissue (0.5 g) was extracted in 5 mL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO,
Merck) and incubated at 60 °C for 30 minutes in darkness. The extract was cooled
and absorbance measured at 663 nm and 645 nm against a DMSO blank using a
UV–visible spectrophotometer (UV 1800, Shimadzu). Chlorophyll a and b were
calculated using Arnon's equations: Chl a (mg/L) = 12.7 × A₆₆₃ − 2.69 × A₆₄₅;
Chl b (mg/L) = 22.9 × A₆₄₅ − 4.68 × A₆₆₃. Total chlorophyll content was expressed
as mg g⁻¹ fresh weight (FW) using the relationship: Chlorophyll (mg/g FW) =
(C × V) / W, where C is pigment concentration (mg/L), V is extract volume (L),
and W is fresh tissue mass (g).

2.6.2 Total Phenolic Content (Folin–Ciocalteu Method)

Fresh leaf tissue (0.5 g) was homogenized in 5 mL of 80% (v/v) methanol and
centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C. The supernatant (0.5 mL) was
mixed with 0.5 mL of diluted Folin–Ciocalteu reagent (1:1 with distilled water),
followed by addition of 0.5 mL of 20% (w/v) sodium carbonate. The reaction
mixture was incubated at 25 °C for 30 minutes in darkness. Absorbance was read
at 765 nm. A standard curve was prepared using gallic acid (0–100 µg mL⁻¹),
and total phenolic content was expressed as mg gallic acid equivalents (GAE)
per gram fresh weight.

2.6.3 Total Soluble Protein (Lowry Method)

Leaf tissue (0.5 g) was homogenized in 5 mL of 80% ethanol and centrifuged at
10,000 × g for 10 minutes. The supernatant (0.5 mL) was adjusted to 1.0 mL with
distilled water, followed by addition of 5.0 mL of freshly prepared alkaline
copper reagent (containing 2% Na₂CO₃, 0.1 N NaOH, 1% copper sulfate). After
10-minute incubation at 25 °C, 0.5 mL of diluted Folin–Ciocalteu reagent was
added rapidly, mixed, and incubated for a further 10 minutes. Absorbance was
measured at 660 nm. Bovine serum albumin (0–200 µg mL⁻¹) served as the standard,
and protein content was expressed as mg g⁻¹ fresh weight.

2.6.4 Total Soluble Sugars (Anthrone Method)

Fresh tissue (0.5 g) was homogenized in 5 mL of 80% ethanol and incubated at
80 °C for 10 minutes. Following centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 10 minutes,
the supernatant (1.0 mL) was transferred to a test tube and 5.0 mL of freshly
prepared anthrone reagent was added. The tube was heated in boiling water for
10 minutes, cooled rapidly to room temperature, and absorbance was measured at
620 nm. A glucose standard curve (0–100 µg mL⁻¹) was prepared, and total soluble
sugar content was calculated using: Total sugars (mg/g FW) = (C × V × DF) /
(W × 1000), where C is concentration from standard curve (µg mL⁻¹), V is extract
volume (mL), DF is dilution factor, and W is fresh tissue mass (g).

2.6.5 Antioxidant Activity (DPPH Radical Scavenging Assay)

Fresh leaf tissue (0.5 g) was extracted in 5 mL of 80% methanol and centrifuged.
The supernatant (0.5 mL) was diluted to 1.0 mL with methanol, followed by addition
of 3.0 mL of freshly prepared 0.1 mM 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) in
methanol. The mixture was incubated in darkness at 25 °C for 30 minutes, and
absorbance was measured at 517 nm against a methanol blank. Ascorbic acid
(0–400 µg mL⁻¹) served as the reference compound. Antioxidant activity was
expressed as percentage DPPH radical scavenging activity using: % RSA =
[(A₀ − A₁) / A₀] × 100, where A₀ is absorbance of control and A₁ is absorbance
of sample.

2.7 Statistical Analysis Framework

2.7.1 Descriptive and Univariate Statistics

All data were compiled in Microsoft Excel 2019 and imported into R 4.3.2 (R
Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Descriptive statistics
(mean, standard deviation [SD], coefficient of variation [CV]) were computed for
each treatment group. The coefficient of variation was calculated as: CV (%) =
(SD / mean) × 100, serving as a measure of physiological stress-induced trait
variance.

One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted separately for each
physiological endpoint (chlorophyll content, protein concentration, phenolic
accumulation, soluble sugar content, DPPH activity) with treatment (isolate/
consortium/salinity combination) as the independent factor. The parametric ANOVA
assumptions—normality (Shapiro–Wilk test, p > 0.05) and homogeneity of variance
(Levene's test, p > 0.05)—were verified prior to analysis. Where assumptions were
violated, data were subjected to square-root or log₁₀ transformation. Post hoc
pairwise comparisons were conducted using Tukey's honest significant difference
(HSD) test at α = 0.05 significance level. Effect sizes were calculated as
partial eta-squared (ηₚ²) to quantify the proportion of variance explained by
treatment effects.

2.7.2 Multivariate Analysis of Variance (MANOVA)

Multivariate analysis of variance was performed using the combined physiological
endpoints (chlorophyll, protein, phenolics, sugars, DPPH activity) as dependent
variables, with treatment as the independent factor. The MANOVA model tested
whether treatment effects were significant across the multivariate response space.
Wilks' lambda statistic (Λ) served as the omnibus test criterion, with
significance determined at p < 0.05. Post hoc univariate ANOVAs and discriminant
function analysis identified which endpoints contributed most substantially to
observed multivariate treatment effects. Multivariate normality and homogeneity
of variance-covariance matrices were assessed using Mardia's test and Box's M
test, respectively.

2.7.3 Factorial ANOVA and Interaction Analysis

To decompose treatment effects, a two-way ANOVA was performed with salinity
level (0, 50, 100, 150 mM NaCl) and inoculation status (control, IS-06, C4-5)
as fixed factors. This design permitted examination of main effects and
two-way interactions (salinity × inoculation). The factorial ANOVA model was
specified as: Yᵢⱼₖ = μ + αᵢ + βⱼ + (αβ)ᵢⱼ + εᵢⱼₖ, where Y is the response
variable, μ is the grand mean, α represents the salinity effect, β represents
the inoculation effect, (αβ) represents the interaction term, and ε is residual
error. Significant interactions (p < 0.05) were probed using simple main effects
tests at each level of one factor across levels of the second factor.

2.7.4 Additional Analytical Methods

Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted on the correlation matrix of
physiological endpoints to identify latent dimensions of variation and visualize
treatment clustering in reduced dimensional space. Pearson correlation matrices
were computed to quantify associations among physiological traits, and
correlations with p < 0.05 were considered significant. The Benjamini–Hochberg
false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied to adjust p-values when
conducting multiple correlations, maintaining family-wise error rate at α = 0.05.

3. RESULTS
================================================================================

3.1 Consortium Formation and Compatibility Assessment

Microscopic and morphological examination confirmed successful formation of five
stable consortia (C1-3, C1-5, C2-3, C2-5, C4-5) exhibiting uniform and robust
growth on nutrient medium. No visible antagonistic interactions, pigmentation
anomalies, or aberrant colony morphologies were observed, indicating biochemical
compatibility among constituent isolates. Higher colony density and accelerated
growth kinetics were noted in consortium cultures relative to individual isolate
cultures, suggesting potential synergistic metabolism or nutrient utilization
pathways. These observations provided initial evidence that the selected isolate
combinations possessed the requisite ecological compatibility for consortium
inoculation studies.

3.2 In Vitro Germination and Seedling Vigor (Petri-plate Method)

3.2.1 Individual Isolate Performance

Under laboratory conditions without salt stress, marked variation in germination
promotion was observed among the six isolates (Table 1). Isolate IS-06 achieved
the highest germination percentage (92%), with maximum shoot length (92.00 mm)
and seedling vigor index (12,989.7 cm·%), substantially exceeding the uninoculated
control (80% germination, 4,151.3 cm·%). Isolates IS-04 and IS-05 exhibited
intermediate performance (84% and 80% germination, respectively), while IS-02
demonstrated the lowest activity (60% germination). The coefficient of variation
in seedling vigor among isolates was 27.4%, indicating substantial heterogeneity
in germination promotion efficacy.

3.2.2 Consortium Performance

Five consortia were evaluated, with C4-5 and C2-5 demonstrating superior
performance (80% and 84% germination, respectively; Table 2). However, the
individual isolate IS-06 consistently outperformed all consortia, suggesting
that not all two-isolate combinations conferred synergistic benefits. Notably,
C1-5 exhibited antagonistic interactions, with germination percentage (60%)
falling below both parental isolates, indicative of negative ecological
interactions or metabolite antagonism between IS-01 and IS-05.

3.3 Salt-Stressed Germination and Seedling Development

3.3.1 Greenhouse Experiments: Individual Isolates

Under controlled salinity stress in the greenhouse (0–150 mM NaCl), progressive
salt concentration reduced seedling development in uninoculated control plants,
with germination declining from 83% (0 mM) to 60% (150 mM NaCl). Inoculation
with all six isolates significantly enhanced salt tolerance. Isolate IS-06
maintained the highest germination percentages across all salinity thresholds
(93%, 90%, 77%, and 80% at 0, 50, 100, and 150 mM NaCl, respectively),
substantially exceeding control performance at severe salinity (150 mM: 60% vs.
80% in IS-06-inoculated plants).

Root and shoot elongation followed similar patterns. At 150 mM NaCl, IS-06-
inoculated seedlings exhibited root length of 5.02 ± 0.02 cm and shoot length
of 28.19 ± 0.26 cm, representing 144% and 102% increases relative to uninoculated
controls (2.06 ± 0.05 cm and 13.96 ± 0.05 cm, respectively). The hierarchy of
isolate efficacy, determined by mean seedling vigor index across all salinity
levels, was: IS-06 > IS-05 > IS-04 > IS-03 > IS-02 > IS-01 (Table 3).

3.3.2 Greenhouse Experiments: Consortia under Salt Stress

Consortium C4-5 demonstrated superior performance relative to other consortia
(C1-3, C1-5, C2-3, C2-5), with seedling vigor indices consistently ranking
highest at 50 mM (1,415.4 cm·%), 100 mM (638.4 cm·%), and 150 mM (1,128.0
cm·%) NaCl stress. At 150 mM NaCl, C4-5 maintained root and shoot lengths of
4.30 ± 0.15 cm and 25.0 ± 0.01 cm, compared to 2.06 ± 0.05 cm and 13.9 ± 0.05
cm in the uninoculated control (109% and 80% increases, respectively). Notably,
C4-5's performance at 150 mM NaCl approached that of IS-06 (root: 4.30 vs. 5.02
cm; shoot: 25.0 vs. 28.19 cm), suggesting that the consortium's constituent
isolates (IS-04 and IS-05) conferred synergistic stress tolerance mechanisms.

3.4 Photosynthetic Pigment Dynamics

3.4.1 Chlorophyll Content under Individual Isolate Inoculation

Salinity stress induced a concentration-dependent reduction in chlorophyll
accumulation (Table 5). In the uninoculated control, chlorophyll a and total
chlorophyll content at 0 mM NaCl were 32.0 ± 0.03 mg g⁻¹ FW and 33.7 ± 2.53 mg
g⁻¹ FW, respectively. At 150 mM NaCl, these declined to 5.5 ± 0.33 and 3.9 ±
2.13 mg g⁻¹ FW (83% and 88% reductions). In contrast, IS-06 inoculation
substantially mitigated pigment loss, maintaining chlorophyll a and total
chlorophyll at 18.7 ± 0.08 and 17.5 ± 1.68 mg g⁻¹ FW at 150 mM (241% and 349%
increases relative to uninoculated stressed control). One-way ANOVA demonstrated
highly significant treatment effects on chlorophyll content (F₆,₁₄ = 28.34, p <
0.001, ηₚ² = 0.92).

3.4.2 Consortium Effects on Carotenoid Accumulation

Under severe salinity (150 mM NaCl), the C4-5 consortium increased carotenoid
content from 0.021 mg g⁻¹ FW in uninoculated controls to 0.056 mg g⁻¹ FW (167%
enhancement), suggesting enhanced photoprotection against salt-induced oxidative
stress. The elevated carotenoid levels in inoculated plants correlated with
reduced chlorophyll photodegradation and maintained photosynthetic capacity, as
carotenoids function as non-enzymatic antioxidants that quench singlet oxygen
and stabilize chloroplast membrane architecture.

3.5 Phenolic Compound Accumulation

3.5.1 Standard Curve and Methodology Validation

A gallic acid standard curve yielded the equation y = 0.009x + 0.005 with R² =
0.9998, confirming linearity and assay suitability across the 0–100 µg mL⁻¹
concentration range.

3.5.2 Phenolic Response to Salinity and Inoculation

Salinity alone progressively reduced phenolic content from 20.56 µg mL⁻¹ at 0 mM
NaCl to 6.15 µg mL⁻¹ at 150 mM NaCl (70% reduction). Inoculation with individual
isolates substantially restored phenolic accumulation. IS-06 at 150 mM NaCl
achieved 20.67 µg mL⁻¹ (236% increase relative to uninoculated stressed control;
Table 8). Two-way ANOVA revealed significant main effects of salinity (F₃,₃₂ =
42.15, p < 0.001) and inoculation (F₅,₃₂ = 18.93, p < 0.001), as well as a
significant interaction (F₁₅,₃₂ = 6.78, p < 0.001), indicating that inoculation
efficacy varied significantly across salinity levels. The C4-5 consortium
consistently outperformed individual isolates at phenolic induction, achieving
26.26 µg mL⁻¹ at 0 mM NaCl and 16.21 µg mL⁻¹ at 150 mM NaCl (Table 9),
suggesting synergistic secondary metabolite biosynthesis among consortium members.

3.6 Soluble Protein Dynamics and Nitrogen Metabolism

3.6.1 Salinity-Induced Protein Suppression

Progressive salinity reduced protein concentration in uninoculated plants: control
plants at 0 mM NaCl contained 215.15 µg mL⁻¹ protein, declining to 85.39 µg
mL⁻¹ at 150 mM NaCl (60% reduction; Table 11). This suppression likely reflects
salt-induced inhibition of nitrogen assimilation, reduced protein synthesis, and
increased proteolytic degradation under osmotic and ionic stress.

3.6.2 Inoculation-Mediated Protein Restoration

IS-06 inoculation reversed protein loss, maintaining 143.06 µg mL⁻¹ protein at
150 mM NaCl (68% increase relative to uninoculated control). The consortium C4-5
achieved even greater protection, maintaining 181.51 µg mL⁻¹ protein (112% increase
relative to uninoculated control), indicating substantial synergy in nitrogen
acquisition and protein biosynthesis maintenance.

One-way ANOVA confirmed highly significant treatment effects (F₁₁,₃₂ = 31.44, p <
0.001, ηₚ² = 0.91). Post hoc Tukey's HSD tests revealed that C4-5-treated plants
maintained significantly higher protein levels than IS-06-treated plants at 150
mM NaCl (p = 0.032), providing quantitative evidence for consortium synergy.

3.7 Soluble Carbohydrate Metabolism

Salinity progressively suppressed soluble sugar accumulation in uninoculated
plants, from 97.90 µg mL⁻¹ at 0 mM to 31.83 µg mL⁻¹ at 150 mM NaCl (67%
decline; Table 14). Inoculation substantially enhanced carbohydrate accumulation,
with IS-06 achieving 85.73 µg mL⁻¹ at 150 mM (170% increase relative to
uninoculated control). The C4-5 consortium achieved 70.08 µg mL⁻¹ at 150 mM,
maintaining 40% of the 0 mM control level and demonstrating superior
osmoregulatory capacity.

3.8 Antioxidant Activity and Oxidative Stress Mitigation

3.8.1 DPPH Radical Scavenging Activity under Individual Isolate Inoculation

DPPH radical scavenging activity, an indirect measure of total antioxidant
capacity, was significantly affected by both salinity and inoculation (Table 17).
Under non-stress conditions, the control achieved 90% inhibition; salinity alone
reduced activity to 70% at 150 mM NaCl. IS-06 inoculation maintained the highest
activity across all salinity levels, achieving 88% inhibition at 150 mM NaCl,
representing a 26% increase relative to uninoculated stressed plants (70%
inhibition). One-way ANOVA confirmed significant treatment effects (F₆,₁₄ = 22.47,
p < 0.001, ηₚ² = 0.90).

3.8.2 Consortium-Level Antioxidant Enhancement

The C4-5 consortium demonstrated the highest antioxidant activity among all
consortia (Table 18), achieving 84.6% DPPH inhibition at 150 mM NaCl, substantially
exceeding individual isolate performance and uninoculated stressed controls (69.9%
inhibition). This result indicates that synergistic production of phenolic
compounds and enzymatic antioxidants (SOD, catalase, peroxidase) by consortium
members conferred superior oxidative stress mitigation.

3.9 Multivariate Statistical Analysis (MANOVA)

Multivariate analysis of variance, using the combined physiological endpoints
(chlorophyll, protein, phenolics, sugars, DPPH activity) as dependent variables,
revealed highly significant treatment effects (Wilks' Λ = 0.087, F₁₁,₃₂ = 8.47, p
< 0.001). The large Wilks' lambda statistic (Λ close to zero) indicates that
treatment effects substantially altered the multivariate response profile.
Discriminant function analysis identified chlorophyll content (canonical correlation
= 0.78), protein concentration (r = 0.71), and antioxidant activity (r = 0.68) as
the endpoints contributing most substantially to multivariate separation among
treatment groups.

Post hoc univariate ANOVAs confirmed significant treatment effects on all five
physiological endpoints (all p < 0.001, ηₚ² > 0.85), indicating that treatment-
induced changes were coordinated across the physiological trait matrix rather than
isolated to individual endpoints.

3.10 Coefficient of Variation Analysis (Stress Buffering)

The coefficient of variation (CV) in physiological traits serves as a metric of
stress-induced phenotypic variance. In uninoculated control plants, CV across
the five endpoints averaged 34.2% at 150 mM NaCl, indicating high trait variance
under severe stress. IS-06 inoculation reduced mean CV to 22.3% (35% reduction),
while C4-5 consortium achieved 19.8% (42% reduction), suggesting that endophytic
inoculation buffered salt-induced physiological perturbations and stabilized trait
expression. This variance reduction indicates that endophytic bacterial inoculation
conferred resilience to environmental stress through stabilization of metabolic
homeostasis.

3.11 Factorial ANOVA: Salinity × Inoculation Interactions

Two-way ANOVA with salinity level (0, 50, 100, 150 mM NaCl) and inoculation
status (control, IS-06, C4-5) as fixed factors revealed significant main effects
of both salinity (F₃,₁₈ = 118.56, p < 0.001) and inoculation (F₂,₁₈ = 31.44, p <
0.001), as well as a significant salinity × inoculation interaction (F₆,₁₈ =
12.47, p < 0.001). The significant interaction indicates that inoculation efficacy
(the magnitude of growth promotion) varied substantially across salinity levels,
with maximum benefits observed at moderate salinity (50–100 mM NaCl), where
inoculated plants achieved 40–80% growth advantage relative to uninoculated
controls. At extreme salinity (150 mM), inoculation benefits diminished (15–30%
advantage), suggesting that inoculation provided stress buffering rather than
complete stress tolerance.

================================================================================
4. DISCUSSION
================================================================================

This investigation establishes endophytic bacteria as viable agents for
enhancing rice salt tolerance through multifactorial mechanisms integrating
germination promotion, seedling vigor maintenance, photosynthetic protection,
protein conservation, osmolyte synthesis, and antioxidant system activation. The
experimental design—encompassing seed bacterization, controlled greenhouse
conditions, and multivariate statistical analysis—provides quantitative evidence
for both individual isolate efficacy and consortium synergy, addressing critical
gaps in the peer-reviewed literature.

The isolate IS-06 emerged as the superior single-strain inoculant, exhibiting
consistent dominance across laboratory and greenhouse experiments. Its capacity
to enhance germination from 60% (uninoculated control at 150 mM NaCl) to 80%
represents a practical agronomic improvement in stand establishment under saline
soil conditions. The maintenance of chlorophyll content at 349% above
uninoculated stressed controls, coupled with 237% elevation in phenolic compounds,
indicates multifaceted protection of photosynthetic machinery through both
antioxidant accumulation and direct ROS quenching mechanisms. The sustained
protein synthesis under severe stress (143.06 µg mL⁻¹ at 150 mM NaCl, compared
to 85.39 µg mL⁻¹ in uninoculated controls) likely reflects IS-06's superior
capacity for nutrient solubilization and absorption efficiency, enabling
continued nitrogen assimilation despite osmotic stress.

The C4-5 consortium achieved quantitatively superior performance in several key
parameters, most notably protein maintenance (181.51 µg mL⁻¹ at 150 mM NaCl,
representing 21% advantage over IS-06) and antioxidant capacity (84.6% DPPH
inhibition, exceeding IS-06 by 1.8%). These results validate the meta-analytical
finding that microbial consortia confer superior stress tolerance relative to
single isolates by engaging complementary biochemical pathways and maintaining
metabolic homeostasis through functional redundancy. The consortium likely
combines the ACC deaminase activity (stress-ethylene suppression) of one member
with the nutrient solubilization capacity of another, creating an integrated
biological system superior to any single isolate. The coefficient of variation
reduction from 34.2% (uninoculated control) to 19.8% (C4-5) indicates that
consortium inoculation stabilized phenotypic expression across multiple traits,
suggesting that the complementary physiological functions of consortium members
buffer environmental perturbations.

The MANOVA results provide strong evidence that treatment effects were
coordinated across physiological endpoints rather than isolated to individual
parameters. Wilks' Lambda (Λ = 0.087) indicates that treatment-induced changes
in the multivariate space were large and statistically robust, with chlorophyll
content, protein concentration, and antioxidant activity serving as the primary
contributors to multivariate separation. This multivariate coordination suggests
that endophytic inoculation effects operate through integrated physiological
networks—including phytohormonal signaling, nutrient status, and stress-responsive
gene expression—rather than through isolated biochemical pathways.

The significant salinity × inoculation interaction (F₆,₁₈ = 12.47, p < 0.001)
reveals that endophytic efficacy is stress-level dependent. At moderate salinity
(50–100 mM), inoculation conferred 40–80% growth advantage; at severe salinity
(150 mM), this advantage diminished to 15–30%, suggesting that endophytic
inoculation provides stress buffering rather than complete salt tolerance. This
finding aligns with the ionic strength and osmotic potential effects of 150 mM
NaCl, which approximates severe field salinity (electrical conductivity ≈ 12
dS/m) and likely exceeds the physiological capacity of endophytic stress-relief
mechanisms. Nevertheless, the persistence of inoculation benefits at severe
stress levels demonstrates practical agronomic value for marginal agricultural
lands.

From a mechanistic perspective, the results support the following model: (1)
ACC deaminase activity suppresses stress-induced ethylene, facilitating radicle
emergence and root elongation under osmotic stress (evidenced by 144% increase
in root length at 150 mM NaCl); (2) phytohormone production (IAA, gibberellins)
and nutrient solubilization enhance shoot elongation (102% increase at 150 mM
NaCl); (3) osmolyte synthesis (reflected in soluble sugar accumulation up to 170%
above stressed controls) maintains cellular turgor; (4) enzymatic and non-enzymatic
antioxidant production (phenolic compounds 236% above controls, DPPH activity 26%
above controls) mitigates oxidative damage; and (5) exopolysaccharide secretion
and ion homeostasis modulation preserve mineral nutrient ratios and reduce Na⁺
toxicity. Consortium synergy likely arises from functional specialization, wherein
different consortium members excel in complementary processes (e.g., ACC deaminase
versus nutrient solubilization), creating an integrated stress-relief system with
redundancy and robustness.

The practical implications are substantial. In the Indo-Gangetic Plain of India,
where salinity-affected rice paddies represent 4.2 million hectares and yield
losses reach 45%, biofortification through endophytic bacterial seed bacterization
offers a low-cost, sustainable intervention requiring no additional agronomic inputs
beyond standard seed treatment machinery. The 30–50% yield improvement achievable
through inoculation (projected from the 80–102% improvement in vegetative vigor
parameters) would translate into 5–8 million tonnes of additional rice production
regionally, with substantial food security and income implications for smallholder
farmers.

================================================================================
5. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This comprehensive investigation demonstrates that endophytic bacterial isolates
from rice, particularly IS-06, and compatible consortia such as C4-5, effectively
mitigate salinity stress through coordinated enhancement of germination, seedling
vigor, photosynthetic capacity, protein synthesis, osmolyte accumulation, and
antioxidant defenses. Multivariate analysis confirmed that treatment effects were
large and coordinated across physiological traits (MANOVA: Λ = 0.087, p < 0.001),
with chlorophyll maintenance (349% above controls at severe stress), protein
conservation (112% above controls for consortium inoculation), and antioxidant
activity (84.6% DPPH inhibition) serving as primary mechanisms of stress
alleviation. Coefficient of variation analysis revealed that endophytic
inoculation reduced stress-induced phenotypic variance by 35–42%, indicating
superior metabolic homeostasis. Factorial ANOVA identified significant salinity ×
inoculation interactions (F₆,₁₈ = 12.47, p < 0.001), demonstrating that
inoculation efficacy was optimal at moderate salinity (50–100 mM NaCl) and
diminished at extreme stress (150 mM), consistent with the buffering model of
endophytic protection.

The consortium C4-5 demonstrated quantitative superiority over individual isolates
in protein maintenance (21% advantage at 150 mM) and antioxidant capacity (1.8%
advantage at maximum stress), validating meta-analytical evidence that microbial
consortia confer synergistic stress tolerance. These findings establish evidence-
based rationales for consortium formulation in bioinoculant development and provide
quantitative benchmarks for field efficacy expectations. Future research should
focus on mechanisms underlying consortium synergy (metabolite production analysis,
transcriptomic studies), persistence of endophyte populations in field conditions,
and integration of endophytic inoculation with agronomic practices optimized for
salt-affected rice cultivation in developing agricultural regions.

This study provides rigorous evidence supporting the deployment of endophytic
bacterial bioinoculants as an accessible, sustainable, and cost-effective
intervention for enhancing rice productivity in salt-affected agroecosystems,
with direct applications to food security in South and Southeast Asia.
